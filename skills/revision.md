
# Revision Skill

## Purpose

Revise a strategic document based on findings from the critic stage.

The goal is to resolve material weaknesses, improve strategic coherence, strengthen evidence, address feasibility issues, and preserve valid strategic decisions.

Revision must be evidence-driven and controlled.

The revision stage must not blindly accept every criticism or unnecessarily rewrite the entire strategy.

## Core Principles

1. Address all critical findings.
2. Address major findings or explicitly justify why they remain unresolved.
3. Evaluate each criticism before implementing a change.
4. Do not accept criticism that is unsupported, irrelevant, or based on incorrect assumptions.
5. Preserve strategic decisions that remain valid.
6. Do not introduce unsupported facts, estimates, forecasts, or claims.
7. Do not silently change strategic objectives.
8. Clearly distinguish changes to the strategy from changes to its wording.
9. Maintain traceability between findings and revisions.
10. Avoid unnecessary scope expansion.
11. Do not conceal unresolved issues.
12. Do not claim a problem has been resolved unless the revision actually resolves it.
13. Do not remove inconvenient evidence or risks.
14. If evidence is insufficient to resolve a finding, identify the additional research required.
15. Maintain consistency throughout the entire document.

## Input

The revision stage receives:

- Original strategy.
- Critic report.
- Research report.
- Evidence ledger.
- Analytical report.
- Strategic alternatives.
- Project brief.
- Strategic objective.
- Constraints.
- Existing assumptions.
- Previous revision history, if available.

If any input is missing, identify the limitation.

## Phase 1. Understand the Original Strategy

Reconstruct the original strategic logic.

Identify:

- Objective.
- Strategic direction.
- Target audience.
- Positioning.
- Value proposition.
- Strategic pillars.
- Initiatives.
- Roadmap.
- KPIs.
- Resource requirements.
- Risks.
- Assumptions.
- Strategic exclusions.

Identify the decisions that must be preserved unless evidence justifies changing them.

## Phase 2. Review Critic Findings

Create a complete list of critic findings.

For each finding, record:

- Finding ID.
- Description.
- Severity.
- Category.
- Evidence.
- Strategic implication.
- Proposed corrective action.

Do not omit findings.

## Phase 3. Evaluate Each Finding

Classify each finding into one of the following:

### Accepted

The criticism is valid and requires a correction.

### Partially Accepted

The criticism identifies a real issue, but its scope or proposed solution requires adjustment.

### Rejected

The criticism is unsupported, irrelevant, incorrect, or does not justify changing the strategy.

### Deferred

The issue cannot be resolved using the available information and requires additional research, testing, or a decision.

For each finding, explain the classification.

Rejection must be justified.

Deferral must include a clear explanation of what is missing.

## Phase 4. Prioritize Revisions

Prioritize changes according to:

- Severity.
- Strategic impact.
- Evidence strength.
- Risk reduction.
- Feasibility.
- Dependencies.

Use the following order by default:

1. Critical issues.
2. Major issues.
3. Moderate issues.
4. Minor issues.

Do not spend time polishing minor wording issues while critical strategic weaknesses remain unresolved.

## Phase 5. Correct Critical Issues

Critical findings may require:

- Revisiting the strategic direction.
- Changing strategic objectives.
- Reconsidering target customers.
- Revising the value proposition.
- Replacing initiatives.
- Revising economic assumptions.
- Changing resource allocation.
- Reworking the roadmap.
- Conducting additional research.
- Rejecting the original strategy.

Do not preserve the original strategy merely because it has already been developed.

If the strategic direction must change, explain why.

## Phase 6. Correct Major Issues

Major findings may require:

- Strengthening evidence.
- Adjusting positioning.
- Reprioritizing initiatives.
- Improving feasibility.
- Revising KPIs.
- Improving risk mitigation.
- Clarifying assumptions.
- Changing sequencing.
- Adding validation experiments.
- Revising resource requirements.

Address the underlying problem rather than merely changing wording.

## Phase 7. Address Moderate and Minor Issues

Resolve moderate issues where doing so improves clarity, consistency, feasibility, or decision-making.

Address minor issues when they can be corrected without unnecessary scope expansion.

Avoid revisions that do not materially improve the strategy.

## Phase 8. Reassess Strategic Decisions

If revisions affect major strategic choices, reconsider:

- Strategic objective.
- Selected direction.
- Target audience.
- Positioning.
- Value proposition.
- Strategic pillars.
- Initiatives.
- Resource allocation.
- Roadmap.
- KPIs.

Determine whether the revised strategy remains coherent.

If a change invalidates another decision, revise the dependent decision as well.

## Phase 9. Maintain Evidence Integrity

When revising claims:

- Use existing verified evidence where sufficient.
- Refer to the evidence ledger.
- Do not invent new sources.
- Do not fabricate statistics.
- Do not upgrade confidence without justification.
- Clearly label new assumptions.
- Identify claims requiring further verification.

If additional research is necessary, specify the research task.

Do not pretend the research has already been completed.

## Phase 10. Maintain Strategic Consistency

Check that revisions do not introduce contradictions.

Review:

- Objectives.
- Target audience.
- Positioning.
- Value proposition.
- Strategic pillars.
- Initiatives.
- Roadmap.
- KPIs.
- Resources.
- Risks.
- Assumptions.
- Exclusions.

Ensure that every major initiative supports the revised strategic objective.

Ensure that the roadmap reflects the revised priorities.

Ensure that metrics reflect the revised objectives.

## Phase 11. Maintain Change History

For every material change, record:

- Change ID.
- Related critic finding.
- Original content.
- Revised content.
- Reason for change.
- Supporting evidence.
- Expected improvement.
- Remaining uncertainty.

Changes may be grouped when several findings affect the same strategic decision.

Do not record purely cosmetic changes unless they are relevant to document clarity.

## Phase 12. Reassess Unresolved Findings

For every unresolved finding, record:

- Finding ID.
- Reason it remains unresolved.
- Severity.
- Required action.
- Additional evidence needed.
- Responsible role, if known.
- Decision deadline, if known.
- Potential impact of proceeding without resolution.

Critical unresolved findings must be prominently disclosed.

Do not imply that the strategy has passed review if critical issues remain.

## Phase 13. Prepare the Revised Strategy

Produce a complete revised strategy.

Do not provide only a list of changes.

The revised document must be independently understandable and must not require the reader to compare multiple drafts to understand the strategy.

Preserve the original structure where possible.

Modify the structure only when it improves clarity or strategic coherence.

## Phase 14. Prepare a Revision Report

Produce a separate revision report containing:

1. Summary of revisions.
2. Accepted findings.
3. Partially accepted findings.
4. Rejected findings and rationale.
5. Deferred findings.
6. Critical changes.
7. Major changes.
8. Minor changes.
9. Change log.
10. Unresolved issues.
11. Additional research required.
12. Remaining risks.
13. Revision completion status.

## Phase 15. Determine Revision Status

Use one of the following statuses:

### Revision Complete

All required changes have been implemented, and no unresolved critical findings remain.

### Revision Complete with Conditions

The strategy has been revised, but some major or moderate issues remain unresolved and must be disclosed.

### Revision Incomplete

Material findings remain unaddressed, or the revised strategy is not internally coherent.

### Strategic Reconsideration Required

The critic findings indicate that the original strategic direction may be fundamentally flawed.

The status must be supported by the revision report.

## Output

Produce:

1. Complete revised strategy.
2. Revision report.
3. Change log.
4. Finding disposition table.
5. Unresolved issues.
6. Additional research requirements.
7. Revision status.
8. Handoff to the critic for independent re-evaluation.

## Handoff

Provide the critic with:

- Revised strategy.
- Original critic findings.
- Finding disposition table.
- Change log.
- Supporting evidence.
- Unresolved issues.
- Additional research requirements.
- Revision status.

The revision stage is responsible for correcting the strategy.

It must not independently declare the revised strategy approved.

Approval must come from the critic's subsequent evaluation or the final validation stage.
