
# Validation Skill

## Purpose

Perform a final independent validation of the strategic deliverable before release.

Verify that the strategy is complete, evidence-based, internally consistent, implementable, aligned with the project requirements, and transparent about its limitations.

Validation is a quality-control stage.

It must not silently rewrite the strategy, invent missing information, or replace the critic.

## Core Principles

1. Validate against explicit requirements.
2. Verify claims against available evidence.
3. Check strategic and operational consistency.
4. Identify missing deliverables.
5. Distinguish verified compliance from assumed compliance.
6. Do not invent evidence or test results.
7. Do not treat document completeness as strategic quality.
8. Do not treat a polished presentation as proof of correctness.
9. Do not silently modify the strategy.
10. Report failures honestly.
11. Identify issues that require human judgment.
12. A failed check must not be hidden by an overall positive assessment.

## Input

The validation stage receives:

- Project brief.
- Original requirements.
- Research report.
- Evidence ledger.
- Analytical report.
- Strategic alternatives.
- Final revised strategy.
- Critic report.
- Revision report.
- Change log.
- Roadmap.
- KPI definitions.
- Supporting files.
- Implementation artifacts, if applicable.

If a required input is missing, report it.

## Phase 1. Validate Requirements

Extract all explicit project requirements.

Classify them as:

- Mandatory.
- Recommended.
- Optional.

Create a requirement checklist.

For each requirement, record:

- Requirement ID.
- Description.
- Required deliverable.
- Evidence of compliance.
- Status.
- Comments.

Use the following statuses:

### Pass

The requirement has been verified as satisfied.

### Fail

The requirement has been verified as not satisfied.

### Partial

The requirement is only partially satisfied.

### Not Verified

The available evidence is insufficient to determine compliance.

### Not Applicable

The requirement does not apply, with justification.

Do not mark a requirement as Pass merely because the strategy claims to satisfy it.

## Phase 2. Validate Evidence and Claims

Review the final strategy for factual claims.

For each material claim, verify:

- A source exists.
- The source is accessible or otherwise available for inspection.
- The source supports the claim.
- The claim is not stronger than the evidence.
- The source is sufficiently relevant.
- The source date is appropriate.
- The claim is correctly attributed.
- Contradictory evidence is acknowledged where relevant.

Check:

- Market statistics.
- Customer claims.
- Competitor claims.
- Industry trends.
- Financial estimates.
- Forecasts.
- Regulatory statements.
- Technology claims.
- Strategic assertions.

Classify material claims as:

- Verified.
- Partially verified.
- Unsupported.
- Contradicted.
- Assumption.
- Projection.
- Hypothesis.

Do not classify an assumption as a verified fact.

## Phase 3. Validate Strategic Coherence

Check whether the strategy forms a consistent logical system.

Verify alignment between:

- Strategic problem.
- Objective.
- Target audience.
- Market.
- Value proposition.
- Positioning.
- Strategic direction.
- Strategic pillars.
- Initiatives.
- Roadmap.
- KPIs.
- Resources.
- Risks.

Identify contradictions.

Examples:

- The objective requires rapid growth, but the initiatives focus only on operational cost reduction.
- The target customer is broad, but the value proposition addresses a narrow niche.
- The strategy prioritizes expansion, but resources only support the existing market.
- The roadmap includes activities without corresponding strategic initiatives.
- KPIs measure activity rather than strategic outcomes.

## Phase 4. Validate Strategic Choices

Check that:

- The selected strategy addresses the defined problem.
- The alternatives were meaningfully different.
- The rationale for selection is documented.
- Rejected alternatives have defensible explanations.
- Important trade-offs are explicit.
- Strategic exclusions are documented.
- The strategy is sufficiently focused.
- Critical assumptions are visible.

Determine whether the final strategy is consistent with the conclusions of the analysis and options stages.

## Phase 5. Validate Implementation

Check whether the strategy can be translated into action.

Verify:

- Initiatives are defined.
- Initiatives have clear purposes.
- Expected outcomes are identified.
- Dependencies are documented.
- Resource requirements are addressed.
- Capability gaps are identified.
- Responsibilities are assigned where possible.
- Roadmap sequencing is logical.
- Decision gates are defined.
- Major implementation risks have mitigations.

Identify initiatives that are vague, incomplete, or impossible to evaluate.

## Phase 6. Validate KPIs

Check each KPI for:

- Clear definition.
- Relevant strategic objective.
- Calculation method.
- Data source.
- Measurement frequency.
- Baseline.
- Target.
- Responsible role.
- Appropriate interpretation.

Identify:

- Missing baselines.
- Unsupported targets.
- Ambiguous metrics.
- Vanity metrics.
- Missing leading indicators.
- Missing guardrails.
- Metrics that create conflicting incentives.

If information is missing, report it rather than inventing it.

## Phase 7. Validate Risks

Verify that:

- Major strategic risks are identified.
- Critical assumptions are considered.
- Risks have appropriate mitigations.
- Early warning indicators are defined where possible.
- Contingency actions exist for important failure scenarios.
- Risks are consistent with the selected strategy.
- Unresolved risks are disclosed.

Determine whether the strategy is excessively optimistic.

## Phase 8. Validate Economic Logic

Where financial information is available, verify:

- Calculations.
- Formulas.
- Assumptions.
- Revenue logic.
- Cost logic.
- Investment requirements.
- Unit economics.
- Financial projections.
- Scenario assumptions.

Check that numerical conclusions follow from the inputs.

Do not invent missing financial data.

Where economics cannot be validated, identify the missing information and the decisions affected.

## Phase 9. Validate Critic and Revision Closure

Review the critic report and revision report.

Verify:

- Every critical finding has been addressed.
- Major findings have been addressed or explicitly justified.
- Rejected findings have a rationale.
- Deferred findings have a documented action.
- Changes are traceable.
- The revised strategy reflects the documented changes.
- No unresolved critical issue is hidden.
- The critic's verdict is accurately represented.

Do not independently declare critic findings resolved without checking the actual revision.

## Phase 10. Validate Document Quality

Check:

- Structure.
- Readability.
- Terminology.
- Consistency.
- Tables.
- Charts.
- Citations.
- References.
- Dates.
- Formatting.
- Executive summary.
- Completeness.

Ensure the document is understandable to its intended audience.

Avoid unnecessary jargon.

Ensure that recommendations are clearly distinguished from evidence and assumptions.

## Phase 11. Validate Supporting Artifacts

Where applicable, verify that supporting artifacts exist and are consistent with the strategy.

Examples:

- Research report.
- Evidence ledger.
- Financial model.
- Roadmap.
- KPI dashboard.
- Implementation plan.
- Presentation.
- Demo.
- Technical documentation.
- Project files.

Do not claim that a file, application, demo, or feature exists unless it has been verified.

## Phase 12. Evaluate Project-Specific Rubric

If the project has an explicit evaluation rubric, assess the deliverable against it.

For each criterion:

- Criterion.
- Maximum score.
- Evidence.
- Assessment.
- Score, if justified.
- Improvement required.

Use the actual rubric.

Do not invent criteria or weights.

If no rubric is provided, use a clearly labeled internal quality assessment rather than pretending it is the official evaluation.

## Phase 13. Identify Remaining Issues

Classify remaining issues as:

### Critical

Prevents release or makes the deliverable unreliable.

### Major

Substantially affects quality or implementation.

### Moderate

Should be addressed but does not prevent release.

### Minor

Limited improvement.

For every issue, specify:

- Description.
- Severity.
- Evidence.
- Consequence.
- Recommended action.
- Whether human review is required.

## Phase 14. Determine Final Status

Use one of the following statuses:

### Ready

Mandatory requirements are satisfied, critical issues are resolved, and the deliverable is sufficiently validated.

### Ready with Conditions

The deliverable is usable, but specified limitations or non-critical issues remain.

### Not Ready

Mandatory requirements are not satisfied, critical issues remain, or the deliverable cannot be reliably validated.

### Human Review Required

A material decision, evidence gap, risk, or requirement requires human judgment.

The final status must be justified.

## Output

Produce a final validation report containing:

1. Overall status.
2. Requirement checklist.
3. Evidence verification results.
4. Strategic coherence assessment.
5. Strategic choice assessment.
6. Implementation assessment.
7. KPI assessment.
8. Risk assessment.
9. Economic validation.
10. Critic and revision closure.
11. Document quality assessment.
12. Supporting artifact verification.
13. Rubric assessment, if applicable.
14. Remaining issues.
15. Human review requirements.
16. Recommended next actions.

## Handoff

Provide the final deliverable with:

- Final strategy.
- Validation report.
- Requirement checklist.
- Evidence status.
- Remaining limitations.
- Unresolved issues.
- Final readiness status.

Validation is responsible for verifying the deliverable.

It must not claim that unverified requirements, implementations, experiments, or evidence have been completed.
