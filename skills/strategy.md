
# Strategy Skill

## Purpose

Develop a coherent, evidence-based, actionable strategy by synthesizing research, analysis, and strategic alternatives.

The strategy must define what the organization intends to achieve, where it will compete, how it will create value, how it will win, what resources it requires, and how success will be measured.

The output must be a decision-oriented strategic document rather than a generic business plan or collection of recommendations.

## Core Principles

1. The strategy must directly address the defined strategic problem.
2. Strategic choices must be supported by evidence, analysis, or explicitly stated assumptions.
3. Clearly distinguish strategic decisions from operational activities.
4. A strategy must specify priorities and exclusions.
5. Avoid trying to pursue every attractive opportunity simultaneously.
6. Strategic objectives must be measurable whenever possible.
7. Recommendations must be feasible within the stated constraints.
8. Do not invent budgets, market sizes, forecasts, KPIs, or capabilities.
9. Explicitly communicate uncertainty.
10. Every major strategic initiative must have a clear rationale.
11. The strategy must be internally coherent.
12. The strategy must be implementable and capable of being evaluated.

## Input

The strategy stage receives:

- Project brief.
- Strategic objective.
- Research report.
- Evidence ledger.
- Analytical report.
- Strategic insights.
- Strategic alternatives.
- Comparative evaluation.
- Risks.
- Constraints.
- Company capabilities.
- Critical assumptions.
- Validation results, if available.

If important inputs are missing, document them and adjust the confidence of recommendations accordingly.

## Phase 1. Define the Strategic Problem

Establish:

- Current situation.
- Desired future state.
- Strategic gap.
- Underlying problem.
- Main constraints.
- Relevant time horizon.
- Key stakeholders.

The problem statement must be specific and relevant to the organization.

Avoid vague statements such as:

- "The company needs to grow."
- "The company should innovate."
- "The company needs better marketing."

Explain what must change and why.

## Phase 2. Define Strategic Objectives

Establish the primary strategic objective.

Where appropriate, specify:

- Desired outcome.
- Target audience.
- Market or geography.
- Time horizon.
- Measurement method.
- Baseline.
- Target.
- Constraints.

Separate:

### Primary Objective

The principal outcome the strategy is intended to achieve.

### Secondary Objectives

Supporting outcomes that contribute to the primary objective.

### Constraints

Conditions that must not be violated.

Examples:

- Maximum investment.
- Time limitations.
- Regulatory requirements.
- Operational capacity.
- Risk tolerance.

Do not create arbitrary numerical targets.

If baseline data is unavailable, identify the baseline measurement as an initial task.

## Phase 3. Select the Strategic Direction

Evaluate the alternatives received from the options stage.

Consider:

- Strategic fit.
- Evidence strength.
- Market attractiveness.
- Customer value.
- Differentiation.
- Feasibility.
- Economic potential.
- Resource requirements.
- Risks.
- Time to impact.
- Scalability.
- Reversibility.

Select the preferred strategic direction.

Explain:

- Why it is preferred.
- Which alternatives were rejected.
- Why they were rejected.
- What trade-offs the decision creates.
- What assumptions the decision depends on.
- What evidence could cause the decision to change.

Do not select an option solely because it received the highest numerical score.

Use judgment and explain it.

## Phase 4. Define Strategic Positioning

Clearly define:

### Where to Play

Specify:

- Target customer.
- Customer segment.
- Market.
- Geography.
- Product category.
- Relevant channels.
- Priority use cases.

### How to Win

Specify:

- Core value proposition.
- Differentiation.
- Competitive advantage.
- Customer experience.
- Positioning.
- Sources of value.

Explain why the target customer should choose the organization over alternatives.

Avoid generic positioning statements.

## Phase 5. Define the Value Proposition

Describe:

- Target customer.
- Customer problem.
- Desired outcome.
- Proposed solution.
- Main benefits.
- Differentiation.
- Supporting evidence.

The value proposition must be understandable, specific, and relevant to the chosen segment.

Separate verified customer needs from assumptions requiring validation.

## Phase 6. Establish Strategic Pillars

Define a limited number of strategic pillars.

Normally, use 3–5 pillars unless the project requires a different structure.

Each pillar must represent a major strategic priority.

For every pillar, specify:

- Pillar name.
- Strategic rationale.
- Objective.
- Expected outcome.
- Major initiatives.
- Required capabilities.
- Dependencies.
- Key risks.
- Metrics.

Pillars must collectively support the overall strategy.

Avoid creating overlapping pillars or turning every activity into a separate strategic pillar.

## Phase 7. Define Strategic Initiatives

Translate strategic pillars into concrete initiatives.

For each initiative, specify:

### Initiative Name

A concise, descriptive name.

### Purpose

Why the initiative is required.

### Expected Outcome

What measurable or observable result it should produce.

### Activities

The major actions required.

### Owner

The responsible role or team, if known.

Do not invent named individuals.

### Resources

Required resources, expertise, technology, budget, and partnerships.

Distinguish known requirements from estimates.

### Dependencies

What must happen before or alongside the initiative.

### Risks

Potential implementation obstacles.

### Metrics

How progress and success will be measured.

### Priority

High, medium, or low.

Explain the prioritization rationale.

## Phase 8. Develop the Roadmap

Organize implementation into logical phases.

A possible structure:

### Phase 1 — Validate

- Validate critical assumptions.
- Conduct additional research where necessary.
- Test customer demand.
- Assess feasibility.
- Establish baselines.
- Confirm constraints.

### Phase 2 — Build

- Develop required capabilities.
- Build or improve products.
- Establish partnerships.
- Prepare operational processes.
- Develop required infrastructure.

### Phase 3 — Launch

- Launch the selected initiatives.
- Test positioning and channels.
- Monitor initial performance.
- Identify operational bottlenecks.

### Phase 4 — Optimize

- Improve performance.
- Adjust pricing.
- Improve customer experience.
- Refine operational processes.
- Reallocate resources.

### Phase 5 — Scale

- Expand successful initiatives.
- Increase investment where justified.
- Expand segments, channels, or geography.
- Establish repeatable processes.

Adapt phases to the actual project.

For each phase, specify:

- Objective.
- Key activities.
- Deliverables.
- Dependencies.
- Success criteria.
- Decision gates.

Do not assign arbitrary dates.

If timing information is unavailable, use relative time horizons and clearly state that they require validation.

## Phase 9. Define KPIs

Establish a measurement system aligned with strategic objectives.

Where appropriate, include:

### Outcome Metrics

Measure the strategic result.

Examples:

- Revenue.
- Profit.
- Market share.
- Retention.
- Customer adoption.
- Customer satisfaction.
- Growth.

### Leading Indicators

Measure early signals of progress.

Examples:

- Qualified leads.
- Conversion.
- Activation.
- Engagement.
- Pipeline.
- Pilot participation.
- Customer acquisition efficiency.

### Operational Metrics

Measure execution quality.

Examples:

- Delivery time.
- Product quality.
- Cost efficiency.
- Capacity.
- Reliability.

### Guardrail Metrics

Ensure progress does not create unacceptable negative outcomes.

Examples:

- Customer churn.
- Cost overruns.
- Quality deterioration.
- Risk exposure.
- Customer complaints.

For each KPI, define:

- Metric name.
- Definition.
- Calculation formula.
- Baseline.
- Target.
- Measurement frequency.
- Data source.
- Responsible role.

Do not invent baseline values or targets.

If targets are unavailable, recommend a process for establishing them.

Avoid vanity metrics that do not support strategic decisions.

## Phase 10. Define Resource Allocation

Identify required resources.

Consider:

- Financial investment.
- Personnel.
- Technology.
- Infrastructure.
- Marketing.
- Research.
- Partnerships.
- Operational capacity.

Where financial data exists, estimate:

- Investment.
- Operating costs.
- Revenue potential.
- Expected benefits.
- Return.
- Payback period.

Use transparent assumptions.

If reliable financial data is unavailable:

- Provide a qualitative resource assessment.
- Identify missing inputs.
- Avoid fabricated budgets.
- Suggest a method for estimating costs.

Prioritize resource allocation according to strategic importance.

## Phase 11. Define Risk Management

Identify the most important strategic risks.

For each risk, specify:

- Description.
- Probability.
- Impact.
- Evidence.
- Risk owner, if known.
- Mitigation.
- Early warning indicators.
- Contingency plan.

Distinguish:

- Risks inherent to the strategy.
- Execution risks.
- External risks.
- Assumption risks.

Do not assume that every risk can be eliminated.

Identify acceptable risks and the conditions under which the strategy should be reconsidered.

## Phase 12. Establish Governance

Define how the strategy will be managed.

Specify:

- Review frequency.
- Decision-making responsibilities.
- KPI monitoring.
- Reporting.
- Escalation.
- Resource reallocation.
- Strategic review triggers.

A possible governance structure:

### Weekly

Operational progress and blockers.

### Monthly

Initiative performance and KPI review.

### Quarterly

Strategic progress, assumptions, priorities, and resource allocation.

Adapt the cadence to the project.

Do not impose unnecessary governance on small projects.

## Phase 13. Define Strategic Decision Gates

Identify decisions that must be made before proceeding to the next phase.

Examples:

- Is customer demand validated?
- Is the economics model viable?
- Are required capabilities available?
- Is the pilot successful?
- Is the risk acceptable?
- Is further investment justified?

For each decision gate, specify:

- Decision.
- Evidence required.
- Success criteria.
- Failure criteria.
- Available actions.

Possible actions:

- Continue.
- Modify.
- Pause.
- Stop.
- Scale.

## Phase 14. Define Strategic Exclusions

Explicitly identify what the strategy will not pursue.

Examples:

- Customer segments outside the priority audience.
- Markets outside the initial geography.
- Features that do not support the core value proposition.
- Initiatives with insufficient evidence.
- Activities that exceed resource constraints.

Strategic exclusions protect focus and prevent uncontrolled expansion.

## Phase 15. Test Strategic Coherence

Before finalizing, verify that:

- Objectives align with the selected direction.
- Positioning matches the target customer.
- Strategic pillars support the objective.
- Initiatives support the pillars.
- Resources are consistent with priorities.
- KPIs measure the intended outcomes.
- Risks are acknowledged.
- Roadmap sequencing is logical.
- Dependencies are addressed.
- Exclusions are consistent with the strategy.

Identify contradictions and resolve them.

## Output

Produce a structured strategy document containing:

1. Executive summary.
2. Strategic context.
3. Problem statement.
4. Strategic objective.
5. Selected strategic direction.
6. Rationale for selection.
7. Rejected alternatives.
8. Strategic positioning.
9. Target audience.
10. Value proposition.
11. Strategic pillars.
12. Strategic initiatives.
13. Roadmap.
14. KPIs.
15. Resource allocation.
16. Economic assumptions.
17. Risks and mitigations.
18. Governance.
19. Decision gates.
20. Strategic exclusions.
21. Key assumptions.
22. Uncertainties.
23. Next steps.

## Handoff

Provide the critic stage with:

- Complete strategy document.
- Research evidence.
- Analytical conclusions.
- Strategic alternatives.
- Decision rationale.
- Assumptions.
- KPIs.
- Roadmap.
- Resource estimates.
- Risks.
- Constraints.
- Unresolved uncertainties.

The strategy stage is responsible for making the strategic choices and translating them into a coherent plan.

It must not hide uncertainty or present unsupported forecasts as established facts.
